# Ardmore ThinkSpace — ADA Context Research

## ADA Pivot Overview

The Ardmore Development Authority (ADA) has redirected $1 million from a failed matching-grant
fund into a 24-month entrepreneurship pivot. The strategy is based on the Center on Rural
Innovation (CORI) Strategic Report. The ADA trust board approved a motion on 2026-07-26 (Trust
Meeting) asking the Oklahoma Department of Commerce to amend Rapid Community Response Program
funding to permit using $1M to implement the CORI strategy and operational plan. The trust board
also approved extending the service agreement with CORI through August 30 at no cost.

The pivot was driven by two questions: (1) Will entrepreneurs show up? and (2) Will partners
commit? A third consideration — whether another community could repeat the model — matters to the
Department of Commerce. This third consideration is the core product angle: a productized
AI-powered ThinkSpace offering that can be sold to other rural communities as "ThinkSpace-in-a-box."

The ADA had previously considered acquiring and renovating a building to serve as a business
incubator, but the trust board showed no interest in moving forward with that approach. The
result was a pivot toward spending the funding over two years, guided by the two questions above
plus the replicability consideration.

CORI's contract was extended through August 30, 2026. The operating lead has not yet been hired;
CORI is helping identify them.

## The Three-Phase Model

The ADA $1M budget is allocated across a three-phase entrepreneurship model:

### Phase 1: Ideation / Validation (Months 1–8)
- Focus: Attract and validate entrepreneurial interest in Ardmore
- Budget allocation: ~$320,000
  - Operating lead compensation (2 years, ~1/3 of total budget): $320,000
  - Tools & playbooks (reusable by Commerce): $100,000
  - Outreach & storytelling: $120,000
  - Programming / founder sprint: $180,000
  - Legal/professional services (Jackson, Fox & Richardson): rate rising from $160 to $180/hour,
    offset by COO Amanda Dion's prep work
  - Honey and Vine grant (city loan-to-grant program): $25,000 (ADA funds the city's share)

### Phase 2: Customer Discovery (Months 9–16)
- Focus: Deep customer validation and market research with local founders
- Budget: draws from remaining outreach and programming allocations

### Phase 3: Early Traction (Months 17–24)
- Focus: Demonstrate measurable traction for the Spring 2028 building decision
- Budget: remaining programming funds; all spend must be evidence-gated

## Pending Constraints

1. **Commerce funding amendment is pending.** Nothing is spendable against the $1M until the
   Oklahoma Department of Commerce approves the amendment. Position pilots to strengthen the
   evidence case, not to front-run procurement.

2. **Operating lead not yet hired.** CORI is helping identify the operating lead. Our proposal
   should make the operating lead more effective, not replace them.

3. **Light-structure and rural constraint.** Everything proposed must run without local technical
   staff and be handoff-able. No server setup; laptop-only tooling.

4. **Replicability requirement.** Commerce explicitly cares whether another community could
   repeat the model. This is our product: ThinkSpace-in-a-box with $100k tools & playbooks
   budget line as the natural fit.

5. **Fiscal lead is the ADA; coordination is the Chamber.** We sell into that structure — we
   don't try to replace the operating lead.

6. **Spring 2028 building decision is evidence-gated.** The ADA needs measurable traction,
   which an AI-augmented program is well positioned to produce and document.

7. **Partners already named.** Commerce, REI Oklahoma, SBDCs — complement, don't compete.

8. **CORI extension through Aug 30.** Time-bound; the operating lead identification window
   closes then.
